# Glass Photo Style

[中文版](README.md)

Transform an uploaded photo into a clean, high-resolution rendered-glass miniature with a collectible model feel.

This independent Codex Skill turns storefronts, buildings, streets, landmarks, objects, or products into precision maquettes assembled from glass slabs, glass blocks, and separate glass components.

## Example directions

### Costa Coffee

Preserve the storefront facade, burgundy awnings, sign, planters, furniture, and warm interior light. The ground and backplane may read as thick glass slabs while the sign and letters remain separate and crisp.

### Temple of Heaven

Preserve the three circular roof tiers, blue-green ornament, red architectural body, gold finial, terraces, balustrades, and central axis. Mix clear and frosted glass across the roofs and terraces to emphasize stacked structure and polished edges.

## Visual direction

- Use a light, clean, precise, Liquid Glass-inspired material language
- A ground plane or backplane may be one complete thick glass slab
- Use smooth satin-frosted glass on broad surfaces and clearer glass on edges, doors, windows, and bevels
- Show believable thickness, rounded bevels, restrained refraction, and clean edge highlights
- Preserve the source color hierarchy without washing the scene gray or turning it into saturated crystal
- Keep major parts separate with clear silhouettes, narrow gaps, and contact shadows
- Follow the source photo's actual front, side, interior, and front-to-back relationships; when the source is already near-front, normalize it into a stable front-facing parallel perspective
- Use parallel perspective while correcting wide-angle distortion, slanted architectural lines, tilted bases, and unnecessary perspective convergence
- Keep signs, logos, and lettering as raised opaque components so they remain crisp and readable
- Generate high-resolution images with architectural detail and clear component boundaries
- Use a high-end studio product composition: center the hero object horizontally and show the complete leftmost and rightmost structures
- Leave at least 20% clear space on all four sides; use a larger or wider canvas when needed instead of crowding the model
- Target a complete-model height of roughly 60–70% of the canvas so the subject remains prominent without feeling cramped
- For wide buildings, expand the horizontal canvas instead of shrinking the model excessively
- Use a clean background, soft studio lighting, precise highlights, and clear contact shadows

## Usage

```text
Transform this photo into a clean rendered-glass miniature with a mix of clear and frosted glass, preserving the composition, high-resolution detail, and comfortable top and bottom margins.
```

## Do not

- Do not make the result a normal photo with a global glass filter
- Do not give every part the same transparency
- Do not fuse signage, letters, roofs, walls, or small props together
- Do not use oily reflections, greasy sheen, rainbow crystal, or candy plastic
- Do not hide detail with global blur, fog, haze, heavy bloom, or severe refraction
- Do not make text or logos blurry or unreadable
- Do not crop the highest point, base, leftmost or rightmost structure, or any major architectural element
- Do not force a clearly oblique source into a front-facing or three-quarter view
- Do not preserve a tilted base, skewed horizontal lines, or unnecessary convergence in a near-front source
- Do not allow less than 20% clear space on any side when the subject can be reframed to fit
- Do not solve insufficient margins by enlarging or cropping the model; expand the canvas or reduce the model scale instead
- Do not force every source into a front-facing or three-quarter view
- Do not let the object, base, shadow, or any major component touch the image edge
- Do not make the model so small that it loses visual focus, or so large that it breaks the 20% safety margins
- Do not add noise, oversharpening, or fake detail as a substitute for high resolution
- Do not add unrelated buildings, objects, packaging, a display case, a watermark, or random text
